# A2: CENOP against DEPONS 3.2, quantitative cross-validation

Date: 2026-11-20. Roadmap item A2. Design and bands:
`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-19-a2-cross-validation-design.md`. Full tables and figures,
generated by `python3 -m validation.a2.report`: `A2-parity-report-tables.md` (run 4; earlier
runs are kept in `run1-before-ship-fix/`, `run2-before-ship-sound-fix/` and
`run3-before-eating-cell-fix/`).

## Result

**One metric fails on the current code: mean energy in the ship scenario at day 150. Every
other metric passes or is inconclusive, in all four scenarios.**

- **The failure.** With ships, CENOP's mean energy at day 150 is 4.29 against 4.14 in DEPONS:
  0.16 higher (3.8%), 2.9 standard errors. The band is "within 2 standard errors and within
  0.25", so the size is allowed and the significance is not.
- **Undisturbed Kattegat**, three dispersal modes: population within 6.2% and 1.6 standard
  errors at every checkpoint, mean energy within 0.23, share of animals per 40 km block
  correlated at 0.999, age-at-death distributions indistinguishable (p 0.32 to 0.65).
- **Ships, other metrics:** population within 4.1% and 2.0 standard errors, block shares
  correlated at 1.000, share of animal-ticks with ship deterrence 0.193 against 0.192.

Verdicts (run 4, CENOP at `8e4abf4`):

|---|---|---|---|---|

INCONCLUSIVE means the band was met, but the spread between seeds at that checkpoint is too
large for eight seeds to rule out a difference of the size the band forbids.

## Run 4 and the energy failure

Run 4 repeats all 32 CENOP runs after the eating-cell fix of 2026-11-20 (`7c14588`), which
changes 222 of 8.1 million animal-ticks in 90 days. The Java runs and the bands are those of
run 3. The same seeds give different trajectories, because one changed tick is enough to
change everything after it; run 4 is in effect a second sample of the same model.

Mean energy at day 150, ship scenario, eight seeds each:

| | Mean | SD between seeds | Difference from DEPONS | In SE |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DEPONS | 4.14 | 0.13 | | |
| CENOP run 3 | 4.25 | 0.14 | +0.12 | 1.7 |
| CENOP run 4 | 4.29 | 0.08 | +0.16 | 2.9 |

- **The fix did not cause it.** The two CENOP means differ by 0.04, far inside their spread.
  The verdict changed because CENOP's eight seeds happened to lie closer together in run 4,
  which made the same difference more significant.
- **The difference is real and specific to ships.** It is not confined to the one
  checkpoint. CENOP minus DEPONS, mean energy, run 4 (standard errors in brackets):

  | Day | 100 | 110 | 120 | 130 | 140 | 150 | 160 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Ships | +0.04 (0.8) | +0.10 (3.2) | +0.12 (3.1) | +0.15 (3.5) | +0.10 (2.2) | +0.16 (2.9) | +0.12 (1.6) |
  | Undisturbed, InnerDanishWaters | -0.04 (-0.9) | +0.01 (0.2) | -0.04 (-1.0) | -0.01 (-0.2) | +0.03 (0.4) | +0.04 (0.7) | +0.05 (0.6) |

  From day 110 to day 150, when the population falls from about 870 to about 400, CENOP's
  animals carry 3-4% more energy than DEPONS's with ships, and the same without ships. The
  cause is not known. Population, location, age at death and exposure agree, so whatever
  differs is in how exposed animals gain or spend energy in that period.
- **No band was changed.** Run 3's statement that every metric passes holds for the code and
  the sample of run 3; it does not hold for run 4.

## The ship scenario: three runs

The bands and the Java runs were the same throughout; only CENOP changed.

| Metric | Run 1 | Run 2 | Run 3 | DEPONS |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Share of animal-ticks with ship deterrence | 0.021 | 0.283 | 0.193 | 0.192 |
| N with ships / N undisturbed, day 359 | 0.994 | 0.911 | 0.971 | 0.918 |
| Population, day 90 | 1291.5 | 1285.9 | 1311.8 | 1314.9 |
| Population, day 359 | 89.4 | 81.9 | 87.2 | 85.2 |
| Mean energy, day 30 | 7.86 | 7.86 | 7.95 | 7.98 |
| Verdicts failed | 4 | 4 | 0 | |

**Run 1 (blind) failed: ships stopped sailing.** Each route in the Kattegat ship file is one
passage in a 721-tick cycle: wait at the first waypoint, sail, wait at the last. CENOP
skipped the first wait, so all 5,148 ships sailed at once from tick 0, and then steered each
ship back to its first waypoint at the last waypoint's speed, which is 0 in that file. Every
ship was stranded after one passage; ship noise ended at about day 25. Fixed in `80c6aa5` by
porting DEPONS's `Ship.move()`.

**Run 2 failed: animals reacted too often.** Exposure was 1.45 times DEPONS's, all year.
Three differences from DEPONS's `Ship.calculateReceivedLevelFor` and its day/night rule:
CENOP used a spreading formula for transmission loss unless a setting was switched on,
where DEPONS always uses WestonFlux for ships; it read depth, sediment and salinity at the
animal's cell instead of at the ship's position; and it used a fixed 6-18 h day and a water
temperature of 12 degrees instead of the landscape's sunrise table and 10 degrees. Fixed in
`bbe57bc`.

**Run 3 passed.** It is not a blind test: both fixes were made with the Java results known.
What supports it is that the fixes are ports of specific DEPONS code, each with its own
tests, and that no band was changed.

**The ship effect on population (M6) is weakly determined.** The ratio is 0.971 in CENOP
and 0.918 in DEPONS, a difference of 1.1 standard errors: with eight seeds the comparison
cannot distinguish an 8% reduction from a 3% one.

## What was compared

- **Models.** DEPONS 3.2, the unmodified release, run headless through the Track A1 harness
  (`validation/depons_java`); CENOP at commit `8e4abf4` (run 4; run 3 was `bbe57bc`), default NumPy/Numba backend.
- **Landscape.** Kattegat, DEPONS v3.2 (CENOP's `data/Kattegat` is byte-identical to the
  files DEPONS downloads).
- **Runs.** 2000 animals, seeds 42-49 on each side. The two models use different random
  number streams, so a seed is a replicate, not a matched pair; only distributions over seeds
  are compared.
- **Scenarios.** Three undisturbed (InnerDanishWaters, the mode DEPONS prescribes for
  Kattegat; PSM-Type2; dispersal off) and one disturbed (ship traffic from the v3.2 ship
  file, 5,148 ships, InnerDanishWaters). Scenario files: `validation/a2/scenarios/`.
- **Day convention.** Day d is the end of day d on both sides.

## Metrics and bands

Fixed on 2026-11-19 in `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-19-a2-cross-validation-design.md` and
coded in `validation/a2/metrics.py` before the runs finished. J and C are means over seeds;
SE is the standard error of their difference.

| # | Metric | Band |
|---|---|---|
| M1 | Population at checkpoints | within 2 SE and within 10% of J |
| M2 | Population trajectory | C inside J +/- 2 SD(J) on at least 95% of days |
| M3 | Mean energy, population-wide | within 2 SE and within 0.25 |
| M4 | Share of animals per 40 km block, days 30-360 | total variation distance at most 0.06, correlation at least 0.98 |
| M5 | Age at death | two-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov test on pooled ages, p above 0.01 |
| M6 | Ship effect: N with ships / N undisturbed at day 359 | within 2 SE |
| M7 | Share of animal-ticks with ship deterrence | within 20% of J |

A pass is reported as inconclusive when 2 SE is larger than the band, that is, when the runs
could not have shown a difference of the forbidden size.

## How blind the comparison was

- **Undisturbed scenarios: not blind.** They were compared repeatedly on 2026-11-18/19 while
  CENOP was being corrected, before any band existed. They are reported with the bands
  applied afterwards and should be read as a record of agreement, not as a test passed.
- **Ship scenario: run 1 was blind except for one seed; runs 2 and 3 were not.** Before
  the bands were fixed, one seed per side had been seen for population (four days), and two
  rough exposure numbers. The analysis code was committed before run 1 finished. Runs 2 and
  3 followed fixes made with the Java results in view.

## Limits

- **One landscape.** DEPONS reference runs exist only for its own landscapes. Nothing here
  validates CENOP on Lithuania or CentralBaltic, whose food layers are placeholders.
- **One backend.** The NumPy/Numba path. The JAX path cannot run InnerDanishWaters, and the
  fused Cython path is used on homogeneous landscapes only.
- **No turbines.** DEPONS ships no Kattegat turbine scenario.
- **Ship reactions are statistically, not draw-for-draw, equivalent.** CENOP draws each
  ship's reaction numbers from its own stream; only rates can match.
- **Seed spread is large.** With 2000 starting animals, year-end populations differ between
  seeds by 3-8 animals (SD). Two false alarms on 2026-11-19 came from comparing against two
  Java seeds, and a third from comparing a population mean with the mean of 200 tracked
  animals. Eight seeds per side and population-wide quantities are the minimum for this
  scenario.
- **Mode of comparison.** Only aggregate distributions are compared. Individual trajectories
  cannot be, because the random streams differ.
